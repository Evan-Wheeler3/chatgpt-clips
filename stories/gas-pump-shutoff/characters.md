# Character requirements

**Decision:** a **new** character. The resolver rejected the approved cooper for this role on two counts:
- **role mismatch:** cooper is not driver;
- **era mismatch:** 1870s, not 2026.

Per the Phase 7A brief, the cooper is not reused as the modern protagonist.

- Request: `characters.json` (slot `protagonist`, role `driver`, added to the `modern` family in `characters/roles.json`).
- Intake: `characters/intake/gas-pump-shutoff-protagonist/`:
  - `brief.md`: the brief for the artist (style rules, palette, required views, expressions, poses);
  - `artwork-request.txt`: a ready-to-use art request;
  - `intake.json`: status **awaiting-artwork**.

## What the character must do
- **Screen time:** about 7 s across shots 1, 2 and 10. The character is the viewer's stand-in; the mechanism is the star.
- **Look:**
  - a modern everyday adult (mid 20s–30s);
  - a zip jacket or hoodie in one strong colour that reads against grey concrete and the pump;
  - jeans, sneakers, mitten hands, a distinctive hairstyle or cap.
- **Expressions:** neutral, happy, surprised, concerned, plus panicked, relieved, curious and impressed.
- **Poses:**
  - holding the nozzle in the filler neck;
  - recoiling;
  - pointing at the nozzle;
  - relaxed, with the nozzle hung up;
  - thumbs-up (an existing preset).
- **Pipeline:** owner-supplied artwork → intake import → **human design approval** → rig → regression baselines → production approval. The same gates as the cooper.

## Owner decisions
1. The protagonist's gender and look.
2. Whether this driver becomes Hold Up's recurring modern everyperson, shared with the 1873 saloon time-traveller. **Recommended: yes, one character.** That's one approval and one rig, and a face viewers recognize across episodes.
