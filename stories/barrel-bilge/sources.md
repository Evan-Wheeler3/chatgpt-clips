# Why barrels bulge: script and source sheet

## Script (original)
> Why do wooden barrels bulge in the middle? That curve has a name: the bilge. Iron hoops squeeze the bent staves tight. And the bulge makes a heavy barrel easy to roll and easy to turn, right on the spot.

- **Hook:** the question in the first two seconds.
- **Mechanism:** hoops and bent staves.
- **Payoff:** the bulge makes rolling and turning easy, shown, not only told.

## Facts and sources
Source: Wikipedia, "Barrel" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrel), retrieved 2026-11-14.

| Claim | Source text |
|---|---|
| The bulge is called the bilge | "Barrels have a convex shape and bulge at their center, called bilge." |
| It makes rolling and turning easy | "This facilitates rolling a well-built wooden barrel on its side and allows the roller to change directions with little friction, compared to a cylinder." |
| Staves are held by hoops | "The wooden parts that make up a barrel are called staves … the rings that hold the staves together are called hoops." |
| Iron hoops | "Early casks were bound with wooden hoops and in the 19th century these were gradually replaced by metal hoops." |

"Squeeze the bent staves tight" is a plain-language description of hoops binding curved staves. "Right on the spot" paraphrases "change directions with little friction" and is not a measured claim.
