# Cinematic camera direction

Smoothness comes first, followed by meaningful visual development and then shot frequency. Give the viewer a new action, relationship, scale or point of attention roughly every 1–2 seconds. A continuous five-second move can work when the composition keeps developing. Give brief comic, emotional and explanatory holds a clear reason. Avoid using whip pans, shake, snap zooms or small idle gestures as the default source of energy.

See [shot planning](STORYBOARD.md#cinematic-shot-planning), [character and prop motion](CHARACTER-RIG.md#character-and-prop-motion-standards), [motion QA](MOTION-QA.md) and the [motion lessons log](MOTION-LESSONS.md), which collects what each owner review taught us.

## Reference observations

**Knox Clips’ chicken story about wealth inequality.** Reviewed the opening excerpt from the owner's 17-second screen recording, `ScreenRecording_11-18-2026 08-04-05_1.mp4`. The full Short is outside the reviewed excerpt. Approximate times below refer to the recording.

- Around 0–6 s, the opening changes from the coin pile to a single coin, then puts a chicken and a comparison board in the foreground. The change of focal subject and scale makes the contrast immediately legible.
- Around 6–11 s, the view shifts from street level to above the roofs. A giant hand and successive broad movements of coins, buildings and fields make the cause and effect fill the frame.
- Around 11–17 s, the view widens from a few figures to the larger group, then hands attention back to the action overhead and down into the crowd. The wider view provides time to understand the group before the next action.

Use these general principles with original staging, characters and assets. A strong foreground entrance, a clear handoff of attention and large changes in depth can carry a narration beat. **Direct review still needed** for motion and pacing across the rest of the approximately 73-second Short, for exact cut and hold durations, and for any claims about trajectories, interpolation or camera rigs. The opening review provides qualitative examples only.

## Camera vocabulary

Each instruction below is an original blocking example. `track` in the declarative implementation is primarily a truck, with a pan option. Use `follow` for a moving prop to receive a damped follow. [Storyboard behaviors](STORYBOARD.md#camera-behaviors-toolsstoryboardcameramjs) lists the actual schema values. Compose behaviors in an episode scene when the desired combination exceeds that schema.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dolly in / out | Emphasize a discovery or reveal its surroundings | Translate on a deliberate approach or retreat. Keep the lens fixed initially and hold aim on the critical feature. Ease into a planned landing or carry velocity into the next leg. | Swapping a physical move for a lens zoom loses parallax. A pure centerline approach can bury the detail behind foreground geometry. | Key the camera carrier in world space and animate a separate aim Empty if needed. Inspect near-plane clearance at the tight end. | Dolly from the workbench edge toward the gauge as the needle rises. End close enough to read the red band. |
| Tracking shot | Follow locomotion and preserve geography during a carry, chase or moving mechanism | Travel parallel, ahead or behind on the actor's world route. Carry the actor's speed through turns and allow lead room. Aim at a stable torso or prop anchor. | Copying every head bob, pinning the subject dead center or letting a turn reverse screen direction without a cue. | Use an offset rail or keyed carrier alongside the staged route. Animate a filtered aim proxy independent of gait. | Track ahead and to the left as the worker carries a crate past two pillars. Leave frame-right open for the destination. |
| Orbit / arc | Show an object's form, expose a second subject or explain a spatial relationship | Move on a partial arc with an intentional pivot, radius, elevation and stable up. Keep the arc long enough to reveal a different silhouette or depth relationship. | Orbiting a symmetric prop without changing the view of any feature. Turning past the approved view of a camera-facing character. | Put the carrier under a pivot Empty or on a Bézier arc. Assign aim independently and inspect the arc for walls and aim flips. | Arc from the safe side of a housing toward its exposed gears as the lever engages. Keep the lever in frame until the gear catches. |
| Crane / pedestal | Reveal scale, a lower level or an overhead relationship | Rise or drop vertically, or combine a rise with a shallow travel arc. Let the tilt uncover the next layer while preserving orientation. | Losing the horizon near vertical or drifting into the overhead structure. | Separate height keys from the aim controller. For an overhead endpoint, provide explicit orientation or an up reference. | Begin below a railing at shoulder height, rise over it and tilt down to expose the floor below. |
| Push-through reveal | Bring a hidden subject into view through a near layer | Travel through clear space past an edge. Schedule when the near object sweeps aside and when the reveal target becomes readable. | Shooting through a wall or letting the foreground block the payoff. | Draw the carrier path around the edge and check swept clearance, near plane and sightlines to the reveal. | Glide beside a doorjamb and let it exit frame-left as a cart rolls across the open yard. |
| Lateral slide | Compare stations, stages or people while keeping a common axis | Translate sideways at a stable height. Use controlled aim or a gradual shift of focus. Use steady mid-move speed if the surface should read evenly. | Flattening the image with undifferentiated layers or overshooting the second subject. | Key a straight horizontal carrier track and check frame-to-frame travel. Keep a near reference for parallax. | Slide past a shelf upright from an empty tray to the tray that fills when the gate opens. |
| Follow-and-reframe | Hand attention from one participant to the next link in the action | Continue travel while the aim moves from the outgoing anchor via a shared action to the incoming anchor. Overlap visibility so the eye has a handoff. | Switching look targets on one frame, panning before the cue or stranding the new subject at an edge. | Key one aim proxy through both driven anchors, or blend orientation with controlled influence and inspect the evaluated result. | Follow the courier to the counter, let the parcel lead the reframe, then land on the receiving hand. |
| Controlled pan / tilt | Follow a change of eyeline, trace a system or reveal an off-screen result | Rotate from a fixed or moving base around the needed axis. Match angular speed to the action and settle where the viewer can read the result. | Overshooting, spinning across the Euler wrap or panning past information too fast to recognize. | Use a separate swivel for simple axes, or sample quaternion orientation for a compound move. Choose a stable up reference. | Hold the camera at the post and tilt from the moving pulley down to the load as tension reaches the rope. |
| Wide-to-detail progression | Establish the geometry before showing a small contact or mechanism | Travel on a clear path into detail, or cut when the contact motivates it. Keep an identifiable landmark or action vector through the change. | Losing orientation, skipping the cause, or doing both a fast dolly and a zoom on the same beat without a purpose. | Block both framings at the output aspect. Use a carrier and aim proxy for the move, or distinct marked cameras for the cut. | Start wide enough to connect hand, lever and machine. Move near the latch for the click while the hand stays on the same side. |
| Action-matched cut | Change coverage while preserving motion the viewer is already following | Cut within a readable action. Match pose phase, contact, prop state, action direction and eyeline. Give the new angle an identifiable purpose. | Restarting the movement, repeating the impact or creating a dead start on the incoming angle. | Bind cameras to cut frames and keep action and contact keyed on the shared scene timeline. Scrub the last outgoing and first incoming frames. | Cut from the worker's shoulder to the landing pad midway through the crate's descent. Finish the same descent and hear the impact once. |

## Implement with the current renderer

- **Declarative.** [`camera.mjs`](../tools/storyboard/camera.mjs) solves framing and samples position, target and FOV. [`easing.mjs`](../tools/storyboard/easing.mjs) supplies the current timing functions. [`sample.mjs`](../tools/storyboard/sample.mjs) combines camera, effects, props and character tracks for validation and rendering. Build with the supported behaviors first. Use semantic framing to establish the shot and evaluate the resolved pose in the actual scene.
- **Hand-directed.** [`tools/episode/render.mjs`](../tools/episode/render.mjs) uses measured words and the story's ordered shots. The episode scene owns exact camera and action choreography. Record pose/path, aim and key action anchors in that scene, then render it through the existing episode entry point. The [seatbelt scene](../stories/seatbelt-locking/scene.mjs) is an example of an action the declarative rig cannot express directly.
- **Both paths.** Keep travel progress, orientation and FOV as distinct controls. Define world-space positions, subject-relative offsets and screen framing explicitly. A world path stays fixed through the set. A subject-relative offset follows a chosen actor frame. A local truck follows the camera's right axis, which may itself turn. Sample the evaluated result at delivered frame times and include effects that shift the actual camera.

## Hand-directed camera toolkit

These techniques were proven in the [Runaway Ore Cart test](MOTION-LESSONS.md#2026-11--runaway-ore-cart-motion-test-storiesmine-cart-motion-test). The reference implementation is in [`stories/mine-cart-motion-test/scene.mjs`](../stories/mine-cart-motion-test/scene.mjs).

- **Key the camera as timed (position, aim, FOV) tuples and interpolate each channel with a C2 natural cubic spline.** Velocity and acceleration then carry through every key. Hermite or Catmull-Rom keys are only C1: in the ore-cart test they produced jerk spikes around 158 m/s³, against about 20 m/s³ with C2. Comment every key with what it shows.
- **Mind the 9:16 horizontal FOV.** Horizontal FOV = 2·atan(tan(vFOV/2)·0.5625), which is only 28° at a 50° vertical FOV. Two subjects spread along the screen's horizontal axis rarely fit. Stage relationships along depth or the frame's vertical axis instead: look down the line, keep the near subject low and the goal high. Widen to 58–62° for reveals and establishing beats.
- **Show danger with height.** A drop reads when the camera sees structure falling away below the edge. A far wall behind the edge flattens it. Add warm atmospheric haze (Three.js `Fog`) to separate near set pieces from distant landforms.
- **Foreground wipe on frame 1.** Start low beside the moving subject, with a near post or rail between lens and subject, so the layer crosses the frame within the first ~0.5 s.
- **Follow a fast prop with a lagged, low-passed aim.** Average the prop's position over the last ~0.15–0.45 s, clamp it vertically, and blend it in with a smooth weight ≤ 0.5. Snapping the aim to a bouncing prop spikes angular acceleration.
- **Pin keys after any dip or crest.** A natural spline overshoots after a large aim change. In the test this cropped the worker at the end until an extra key pinned it.
- **Tune numerically before rendering.** Project the subject landmarks (both ends of the vehicle, the goal, the character's head and feet, the contact point) for candidate keys, and check NDC on every frame. Candidate paths for the ore-cart test were compared in seconds this way, against about 90 s per preview render.
- **Keep the camera moving through impacts.** Sell weight with the subject (pitch, bounce, load surge), not with camera shake.
- **Settle on purpose.** It is fine to bring the closing move below 0.05 m/s; record it as an intended settle in the QA notes.

## Motion engineering and optional Blender authoring

1. **Stage endpoints and path first.** Choose the desired silhouette, screen position, eye line, up vector, focal point, lens, clearances and destination. Sketch world-space waypoints around geometry and mark the visual events the path must expose. In Blender, parent the camera to a carrier Empty for travel. Use a separate aim Empty and reserve a child/local transform for intentional framing or roll trims. Keep scales predictable and check the order of parenting and constraints.
2. **Control time along the path.** Key position for a simple dolly or slide. For a curved move, use a Bézier rail with Follow Path and animate fixed `Offset Factor` or the configured curve timing. Keep Follow Curve orientation off when a separate aiming system owns rotation. Inspect sampled travel. When constant world speed matters, bake or remap progress by cumulative evaluated arc length and apply acceleration only where intended. Linear progress on an arbitrary curve or a change of curve segment can still produce visible speed changes. [Follow Path manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.0/animation/constraints/relationship/follow_path.html)
3. **Match velocities at joins.** Through a continuous action, match outgoing and incoming velocity vectors, including their direction, across position segments. Match angular motion and lens rate where they join too. Shape Bézier handles or explicitly author matched tangents, with no overshoot into obstacles. Ease to rest only at a motivated stop. A continuous tracking move should carry momentum through intermediate waypoints. A deliberate edit can begin in motion. Inspect the whole move, especially where Bézier meets linear, a hold begins, or a constraint changes influence. [F-curve properties](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.4/editors/graph_editor/fcurves/properties.html)
4. **Stabilize orientation.** For ordinary Blender look-at shots, Track To can use camera-local `-Z` aim and `Y` up. Keep the target separated from the camera and avoid an aim direction nearly parallel to the chosen up. Near overhead, author an explicit up/roll solution or use a suitable tracking constraint with roll controlled separately. For baked compound rotations, normalize and sign-align adjacent quaternions and use a shortest-path interpolation where appropriate. For an intended long rotation, add intermediate orientations and inspect angular speed through each join. Blender quaternion keying alone does not guarantee the desired speed profile. [Track To manual](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.4/animation/constraints/tracking/track_to.html)
5. **Check framing and clearance along the entire path.** Test the camera center and near-plane envelope against moving objects and the set. Check intermediate positions between sampled poses for fast movement. Ray-test the aim and additional landmarks such as the hand and contact point. Preview in 9:16 with the actual projection, caption layout and intended occluders. Mark any intentional foreground crossing with its exit and focal exposure. Resolve physical penetrations by reblocking before adding smoothing or blur.
6. **Keep the production handoff deterministic.** Blender is optional for specialized authoring. The current pipeline has no Blender camera import contract. For a future interchange pilot, bake evaluated world transforms on the episode frame range and record frame origin, FPS, units, coordinate basis, FOV/film fit, up and clip planes. Apply the Blender Z-up to Three.js Y-up conversion once and compare projected start, middle, end and contact frames in the receiving renderer. Verify any exported prop or rig animation loads and matches there before relying on it. Use [motion QA](MOTION-QA.md) for continuity across the full shot and through the edit.
