## Phase A: audit of Bram
**Artwork:**
- **Childish construction:** a round head, about a third of his 1.12 m height, button nose, blush cheeks, and huge round eyes with tiny pupils.
- **Competing colours:** seven saturated colours (green cap, ginger hair, peach skin, pink blush, cream, orange leather, blue). The warm skin, apron and hair merge into warm brick.
- **Cardboard construction:** a box torso and tube limbs with square-cut ends, so the elbows and knees show seams. The definition check confirms the ankle pivots sit outside the shin art (`JOINT_GAP`).
- **Line weight:** the outline is baked in millimetres, so it's heavy in close-ups and nearly gone in wides.

**Rendering:**
- **Unstable lighting:** Bram is lit as a lit 3D card plus a self-glow, so his colour changes with camera angle. The studio metric shows ΔE 17.5 between shots, and bloom made him glow under a bright key.

**Animation and rig:** these work. Point, push, walk, occlusion and contact read correctly, and they are kept.

### Evaluation
**A · Minimal editorial**
- *Strengths:* the most adult and premium-looking. It has the most disciplined palette and the steadiest colour, and the dark apron against the light shirt gives a strong two-value silhouette on warm brick.
- *Weaknesses:* the restrained face is the weakest at phone size. Dot eyes and a small mouth flatten reactions, and a "Why…?" or surprised beat lands less. It risks reading as generic stock illustration, with the least channel personality.

**B · Stylized storyteller**
- *Strengths:* the most expressive and best-acting. Heavy lids, brows and a moustache give clear attitudes. It has the largest readable face, and the cool blue-grey shirt against warm brick gives the best colour separation. The weight and the cooper's forearms sell the physical push. It reads as a grown craftsman, not a child.
- *Weaknesses:* the most detail to keep consistent (cel shading, lids, facial hair). At 3.9 heads it is the closest to "cartoony", so proportions need guarding per archetype. Its bald, moustached master is a specific type, and other archetypes must not all inherit that face.

**C · Graphic cutout**
- *Strengths:* the most distinctive and "designed". The bold contour holds best at any distance (highest edge contrast), and the visible brads and square forms make the 2D-in-3D choice feel intentional.
- *Weaknesses:* it has the lowest body-versus-surroundings contrast, and its flat colour is the furthest from the detailed, lit PBR world, so it can look pasted on. Small faces depend on the hat and brows not merging: an early version read as a bandit mask in the wide shot until the hair colour changed. Squared heads can feel stiff in reactions.

### Recommendation (pending human approval)
**B · Stylized storyteller** as the base direction, refined with lessons from the other two:
- **From A:** A's palette discipline (four clothing colours, one accent).
- **From C:** a slightly heavier outer contour (3 → 3.5 px) for wide shots.

The reasoning: Hold Up is narrated explainer storytelling at phone size. The character's job is to *react and demonstrate*, and B measured the largest readable faces, the best separation from warm sets and the clearest acting, while dropping Bram's childishness. **This is a recommendation only.** Nothing has been switched.

**Items any chosen direction still needs:**
- **Restaging:** the adult designs are 1.5–1.64 m, and the barrel-bilge shots were blocked for a 1.12 m apprentice. In the profile shot the adults' heads are cropped, and A's adaptive push bends very low. Restaging belongs to the rollout, after approval.
- **Family defaults:** each family should carry its own proportion defaults. The archetype lineup shows that families currently differ mainly in face, line and shading, while proportions come from the person spec.
- **Coat shoulders:** the shoulder line reads a little like a hood where the coat torso meets the arm, which needs a polish pass.

## Phase D: the Hold Up character specification
These rules apply to whichever family is chosen.

- **Proportions:**
  - Adults are 4.5–5.5 heads tall; storyteller adults may go to 3.9, teens about 4.6. Below 3.3 heads reads as a child (`CHILDLIKE_PROPORTIONS`).
  - Legs are 43–50% of height.
  - Bodies vary by `build` (shoulders, hips, limbs, forearms, belly, legs), not just by clothes.
- **Shape language:** one family per channel look. Limbs are tapered capsules with round caps, so joints overlap at any angle (`JOINT_GAP` enforces this). Hair is cut from the skull outline, never floating slabs. Protruding features (nose, ear, hat brim) get their contour from the silhouette pass.
- **Face construction (3/4 view facing screen-right):**
  - Near and far eyes are offset in perspective. The eye height is at least 7% of head height (`FEATURES_TOO_SMALL`), and features carry extra texture density.
  - The ear sits at the back third; the nose is the signature feature per family. Facial hair is part of the person spec.
- **Expressions:** six shared mouths (neutral, smile, grin, oh, grit, hmm), brows with offset and tilt scaled per family, blinks, eye targets. Each pose preset sets a face, so faces change only with performances.
- **Line:**
  - The outer contour is constant in *screen pixels* at 1920-pixel frame height, whatever the camera distance: 2.2 / 3 / 4.5 px by family.
  - Inner lines are thin, in millimetres. The line colour is a near-black tuned per character, never pure black against skin.
- **Shading:**
  - Flat colour plus at most one cel shade, on the side away from the viewer, clipped to the shape (`clip: true`). The face stays one value.
  - Highlights only on the nose and pupils, and only in the storyteller family.
  - Rendering is flat-tinted: authored colours times a gentle environment tint (key colour 14%, nearby lantern warmth), compensated for exposure. Colours therefore hold across shots (`CHARACTER_COLOR_SHIFT`).
- **Palette:**
  - At most 5 clothing colours (`TOO_MANY_COLORS`), saturation at most 0.62 (`SATURATED_COLOR`).
  - Adjacent big masses (shirt/apron/trousers, skin/shirt) must differ in value (`LOW_VALUE_SEPARATION`).
  - Against warm sets, at least one large cool or neutral mass per character.
  - Skin tones are a range, not a default.
- **Contrast:** at the silhouette edge (aim above 0.3) and between body and surroundings (ΔE above 12), measured on rendered pixels.
- **Pose readability:** every action reads as a silhouette. Presets include anticipation (wind-up, step-in) and follow-through, crouch depth scales with each body, and pushes adapt their reach.
- **Historical clothing:** a garment vocabulary of shirt (rolled or long sleeves), waistcoat or bodice, leather apron, frock or wool coat, long skirt with stockings, trousers, neckerchief, and hats (flat cap, bowler, top hat, bonnet). New periods add garments and hats to the generator, not one-off drawings.
- **Consistency:** a character is a spec. Its definition is generated and committed, and tests fail if a committed definition drifts from its spec. The same definition is used in every shot.

### Archetypes (`characters/archetypes/`)
Archetypes are person templates that work in any family:
- **Apprentice:** teen, flat cap, waistcoat.
- **Scholar:** 1890s chemist, bun, bodice, long skirt.
- **Sailor:** elder, grey beard, wool coat, cap.
- **Merchant:** portly, top hat, frock coat.
- **Laborer:** broad dock worker, rolled sleeves.

They span 1.45–1.70 m, five skin tones, three ages and four body types. See `archetype-lineup.png`.

## Phase E: 2D-in-3D integration changes
Flat-tinted designs only, so Bram's approved Phase 5 rendering is unchanged:
- **Lighting:** flat-tinted shading with consistent response.
- **Line:** a constant-pixel outer outline found from part alpha in the shader.
- **Shading:** clipped cel shading.
- **Faces:** 1.6× texture density on face parts.
- **Expression:** per-family expression range.

For all characters:
- **Grounding:** a two-layer contact shadow (a soft pool plus a stance-aligned tight patch under the feet).
- **Push targeting:** pushes aim at the prop surface nearest the body. Previously they aimed at the screen-space silhouette edge, which could be a barrel's far end cap.
- **Scaled poses:** crouch and walk bob scale with hip height.
- **Adaptive push:** pushes lean, then crouch, then step in, until the hands arrive.

## Phase F: checks (review assistance, not approval)
- **Definitions** (`tools/characters/validate.mjs`): `MISSING_PART`, `UNKNOWN_COLOR`, `JOINT_GAP`, `FEATURES_TOO_SMALL`, `TOO_MANY_COLORS`, `SATURATED_COLOR`, `LOW_VALUE_SEPARATION`, `CHILDLIKE_PROPORTIONS`.
- **Rendered pixels** (`window.characterMetrics`, `tools/characters/metrics.mjs`): each character's silhouette mask is rendered on its own layer, then:
  - `CHARACTER_WEAK_SILHOUETTE` (edge contrast)
  - `CHARACTER_BLENDS_IN` (ΔE versus surroundings)
  - `CHARACTER_DETACHED_PARTS` (more than one silhouette piece)
  - `FACE_TOO_SMALL_ON_PHONE` (head under 16 pt on a 390 pt screen)
  - `CHARACTER_COLOR_SHIFT` (between shots)
- **Plan validation:** existing checks such as `CHARACTER_NO_CONTACT` and `CHARACTER_CLIPPING` now run for any body size.

These checks found the joint gaps and colour drift in Bram, and the palette problems in the first candidate drafts. They did *not* find the hair-slab, sideburn, monocle-nose or bandit-mask problems. Those came from looking at the renders, which is why approval stays human.
